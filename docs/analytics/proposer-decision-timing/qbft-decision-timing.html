Do proposer duties decide after 3 seconds — and does ProposerDelay push them there?

Across a month of mainnet, deciding after 3s is rare (0.11%) and almost always means a QBFT round change — 27 of 28 late decisions are round 2. Round 2 is the hazard (14% miss vs 0.008% for round 1), but it's rare and concentrated in a few chronic clusters. Within the ProposerDelay values actually deployed, delay does not push decisions into the danger zone — only round changes do.

Phase 1 · when QBFT decides

Almost everything decides by ~1.5s — with a second, tighter cluster at ~2.05s

Decision time (exporter's view of commit quorum, relative to slot start) is bimodal. A broad mass peaks at 1.4–1.5s, dominated not by ProposerDelay but by MEV block-fetch — even SSV Labs' own delay=0 nodes land at ~1.1s. A distinct, tight mode sits at ~2.05s: the clusters running a real ProposerDelay. The 3s and 4s lines are almost empty.

Why the ~1.1s floor is block-fetch, not delay

ProposerDelay sleeps until slotStart + delay, then the leader fetches the block and starts consensus. On SSV Labs' operators (ground truth proposer_delay=0s) the block fetch alone took ~1.13s, so the proposal didn't broadcast until +1.15s. Observed lateness ≈ ProposerDelay + block-fetch, and the fetch term dominates.

Phase 1 · the 3-second question, by round

Past 3 seconds, it's round 2 — and round 2 is where blocks die

Round 1 decisions concentrate under 2s and taper out by ~3s; anything later is a round change. Round 2 is rare (0.17% of duties) but its miss rate is three orders of magnitude worse than round 1. Three duties never reached consensus at all — all three missed.

Phase 2 · reading ProposerDelay from the wire

Absolute ProposerDelay can't be cleanly recovered — but the high tier is visible

We estimate a cluster's delay from its round-1 proposal timing (leader-rotation median). The problem: block-fetch varies more than the delay we're hunting. The fastest clusters median ~0.7s (likely local block building); SSV Labs at delay=0 medians ~1.1s. So the 0–400 and 400–700ms buckets are not separable from fetch noise. Only the tight ~2.05s mode — ~21 clusters running roughly 0.9s+ — stands clearly apart.

Reading the result

The danger zone is reached by round changes, not by delay

Failures are chronic-cluster-driven

Round 2 (42 duties) is concentrated: 2 clusters produce 50% of it — one alone accounts for 14. Of 14 total misses, one cluster causes 5 (36%). This mirrors the committee-side finding that reliability is an operator-health problem, not a timing one.

Delay, as deployed, stays clear of the cliff

The highest identifiable delay tier still decides by ~2.05s — comfortably inside the safe zone. Its miss rate (0.04%) is indistinguishable from everyone else's. A ~0.9s ProposerDelay plus ~1.1s fetch lands at ~2.0s; only a round change (+~2s) reaches 3–4s, and round changes track cluster health.

Data-sufficiency — read these numbers with the counts in mind

  • The whole tail is thin: 28 decisions >3s, 42 round-2 duties, 14 misses in 30 days. Per-tier differences sit inside overlapping Wilson intervals — not enough to attribute any effect to ProposerDelay.
  • Only one delay tier is real: the ~2.05s mode (21 reliable clusters). The 0–400 vs 400–700ms distinction is below the block-fetch noise floor and should not be read as a delay signal.
  • Concentration, not distribution: ≤2 clusters drive half of every failure category — so tier-level rates are hostage to which tier those clusters happen to fall in.
Methodology

How the dataset was built

  • Spine (identity + on-chain). Every proposer duty in the window from the E2M duties API (types=propose, with_committee) — slot, validator, committee operator-set, on-chain Success, MEV reward. 25,126 duties.
  • QBFT timing. Per-duty validator trace from the Exporter (network-wide P2P observer): per-round proposal / prepare / commit / round-change and the decideds quorum time. Decision time = decided timestamp − slot start (beacon genesis + slot·12s). 99.63% coverage; adaptive batching with bisect-on-disconnect.
  • Delay estimate (derived). Per-cluster median round-1 proposal offset, calibrated against SSV Labs' known delay=0 (median 1115ms). Kept strictly separate from the timing and on-chain layers.
  • Caveats. Exporter timestamps are receive-times (a few ms behind operators; SSV-Labs-adjacent). ProposerDelay affects proposer duties only. Absolute delay is not separable from block-fetch/source latency.
