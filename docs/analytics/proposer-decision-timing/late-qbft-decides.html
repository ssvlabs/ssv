Late QBFT decides — who concentrates them

Proposer duties whose QBFT consensus is decided late in the slot (when the Exporter receives each cluster's aggregated decided message, relative to slot start). Late decides are almost entirely round changes, they cluster in a handful of chronic operators, and on-chain misses only begin past ~3.5s — so a late decide is usually a warning sign, not a failure.

How to read both cases

  • Late decide = round change. 27/28 (>3s) and 41/48 (>2.5s) are round 2. Round 1 essentially never crosses these lines.
  • Concentrated in chronic round-changers. Two clusters own ~40%; both round-change often yet land every time (0 misses) because they still decide under ~3.3s.
  • Misses are essentially one cluster. All 6 misses (identical at both thresholds) sit above ~3.5s and are confirmed empty slots on the canonical chain; 491, 770, 1002, 1003 — tiny (24 duties), 16.7% late, 100% of its lates missed — causes 4 of them.
  • Not a ProposerDelay effect. Zero late decides come from clusters running >1000ms ProposerDelay at either threshold; the driver is consensus round changes (operator health), not delay.

What the tail says

  • Round 1 finishes fast, or not at all. 89% decide within 100ms and 99% within ~190ms; only 0.02% (5 duties) exceed 1s.
  • Nothing approaches the 2s deadline. The slowest round-1 success was 1148ms — a ~850ms dead zone (1.15–2.0s) holds zero decides. Past ~1.1s a stalled round doesn't "just barely" recover; it times out into round 2 instead.
  • Measured as the Exporter's decided-message receive-time minus the round-1 proposal receive-time, so slot timing cancels; it includes the decided broadcast + one network hop, and sub-~30ms values sit near the Exporter's timing resolution. 4 duties with out-of-order timestamps dropped.
